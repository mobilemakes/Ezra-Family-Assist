# Dialysis Care Standards Reference

> Use this as a baseline to identify when Joseph's care deviates from accepted standards.

---

## Hemodialysis: Expected Standards of Care

### Session Frequency & Duration
- Standard: **3 sessions per week**, typically **3.5 to 4 hours** each.
- Shorter sessions require medical justification and documentation.
- Kt/V (a measure of dialysis adequacy) should be **≥ 1.2** per session (or ≥ 1.7 for twice-weekly).

### Access Care
- Fistulas and grafts must be assessed at each session (bruit, thrill, appearance).
- Catheter sites must be cleaned and inspected per protocol.
- Staff must use proper sterile technique every session.

### Monitoring During Treatment
- Vital signs (BP, HR, weight) must be taken **before, during, and after** every session.
- Blood pressure should be monitored periodically throughout treatment.
- Staff must respond promptly to alarms and patient complaints.

### Labs
- Monthly lab draws are standard for dialysis patients (includes: BUN, creatinine, potassium, phosphorus, hemoglobin, calcium, albumin, Kt/V).
- Results should be reviewed with the patient and care team.
- Patients have the right to receive copies of all labs.

### Medications
- EPO/Aranesp (for anemia), iron, Vitamin D, and phosphate binders are commonly prescribed.
- Medications must match the written prescription; any changes require physician order.
- Medications should not be withheld without documented medical reason.

### Care Plan
- Every ESRD patient must have an individualized care plan.
- The care plan must be reviewed and updated regularly (at minimum monthly).
- Patients and family must have the opportunity to participate in care plan meetings.

## Peritoneal Dialysis: Expected Standards of Care

- Exchanges must follow the prescribed schedule (continuous or cycler-based).
- PD catheter exit site must be inspected for signs of infection at every exchange.
- Cloudy effluent (drain fluid) is a sign of peritonitis — requires **immediate** medical evaluation.
- Training must be provided to the patient and caregiver before home PD begins.

## Red Flags: When to Document and Report

| Observation | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Session ended early without explanation | Inadequate dialysis — raises Kt/V concerns |
| Machine settings changed without discussing with patient | Possible deviation from prescribed plan |
| Labs not shared or explained | Violation of patient rights |
| Medication skipped without reason | Potential harm (e.g., anemia worsening) |
| Staff failed to respond to hypotension (low BP) | Safety failure |
| Access site not assessed | Risk of infection or access failure |
| Patient complaints dismissed | Potential neglect |
| Care plan not updated or reviewed | Regulatory violation |

## Dialysis Adequacy (Kt/V) Explained

- **K** = Clearance of the dialyzer
- **t** = Time on dialysis
- **V** = Volume of fluid in the body

A Kt/V of **1.2 or higher** is the federal minimum standard for adequacy.
If Kt/V is consistently below this, treatment may be inadequate — document and ask the medical director.
